## Why does Geneva need a bilingual yoga website?

Geneva brings together long-time residents and an international community of professionals and families. Some people search for *cours de yoga Genève*; others look for *yoga classes Geneva*. A complete French and English site gives both groups a clear way in.

## How should you structure French and English content?

- Use separate French and English URLs.
- Add hreflang annotations so search engines can identify the alternatives.
- Write each version naturally, instead of translating word for word.
- Keep a visible language switcher on every page.
- Make booking instructions, confirmation messages and policies understandable in both languages.

## Which Geneva searches matter?

Useful topics include *cours de yoga Genève*, *yoga Genève débutants*, *yoga prénatal Genève*, *yoga classes in English Geneva* and neighbourhood-specific searches. Choose topics that genuinely match your teaching and make each page useful on its own.

## What should a Geneva yoga page include?

- Teaching languages and class types near the top of the page.
- A schedule, private-session availability or online booking link.
- Prices in CHF and a clear introductory offer where applicable.
- Location and practical travel details.
- Teacher bios, photos and reviews in the languages your audience uses.

## For solo yoga teachers

For a freelance teacher, a compact site can be the most effective choice: an approachable bio, a simple service list, workshop and private-session booking, online classes or retreats, and a link-in-bio-friendly landing page. It is enough to turn a recommendation or social visit into a direct enquiry without pretending to be a large studio.

## What about data protection and trust?

Explain clearly how your contact form, analytics and booking tools handle visitor data, and publish an accessible privacy policy and business contact details. If you serve people across borders, professional legal review is sensible before publishing final legal copy.
